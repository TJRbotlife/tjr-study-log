## What we learned

Day 43 is a walkthrough of one upcoming week, and he says the procedure is the same one he uses on an ordinary open. He starts with the calendar, then marks building blocks, likely retrace areas, and draws on liquidity, from higher time frames down. If the week proves the read wrong, the bias changes.

On that week's calendar he calls Monday clear, with extra care for GBP pairs around 11 a.m. U.S. time because that print falls in the London session. Another GBP item sits on Tuesday. Wednesday is CPI, and he says he likely will not trade it. Thursday is PPI and unemployment claims, both about an hour before the cash open, so those days are for watching what the print does to price. Friday is a consumer-sentiment release. He says that one matters less to him, and he treats Friday as tradable after the midweek prints. He also says he wants to keep marking levels on days he already knows he will sit out, so the reaction is still studied.

The chart portion is that week's examples, not a standing call. On the S&P he notes a monthly break of structure to the upside that he had not wanted, because of how the fundamentals looked. He takes the print as fact: weekly and daily are bullish, a daily order block is a likely draw, equilibrium has already been tested, and a four-hour break up then a pullback leaves liquidity that Monday might take before a further push. He would look for buys on Monday and Tuesday only while the hourly and four-hour still agree. He treats that daily equilibrium as the weekly equilibrium as well, so the week still looks like a retrace inside a bullish read.

GBPUSD, in his labels, has done the weekly uptrend he expected: a daily break down that filled weekly equilibrium, then a daily break up. The first target he names is the top of an imbalance, then weekly liquidity above. He wants a retrace into the order block left by the recent nonfarm move, which swept liquidity and rallied. GBPJPY is still a long uptrend and has not broken daily structure after taking liquidity. He leaves two paths: a daily break up that continues the trend, or a daily break down that would be the weekly retrace and would flip the daily reads bearish toward an imbalance and equilibrium. Gold is an uptrend on the monthly after taking liquidity, a downtrend on the weekly, and still bearish on the daily without a daily break down. A monthly discount only becomes the active story, he says, after a weekly break back up. Until a daily break even exists, he keeps the bearish weekly read, with a possible pop into equilibrium and an imbalance before lower draws.

## What to remember

- His weekly pass starts with the calendar, then the same top-down marks he uses at an open: building blocks, retrace areas, and draws on liquidity.
- Midweek CPI and PPI are, in this lesson, days he plans to watch rather than trade. A lighter Friday print is not in the same category for him.
- Higher time frames can disagree. Gold in this walkthrough is the example: monthly up, weekly down, and no daily break yet, so the nearer bearish structure still leads.
- A monthly break he did not want is still the structure he has to read. The preference loses to the print.
- The whole weekly map is provisional. A wrong read is replaced when price proves it.

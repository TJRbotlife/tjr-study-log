## What we learned

Day 16 is how to see a fair value gap, after the prior definition. He recaps it as a liquidity void: a stretch with no resting orders against the direction price is moving. Price can return there and react because that liquidity was missing, which is why he calls the gap a retracement tool. He says it is not a reversal tool. In a rising market he looks for bullish gaps that can hold a pullback and continue the move. He is not looking for a bearish gap to turn the trend around. He pairs the same job with equilibrium later: continuation after a retracement, once a bias exists.

The pattern is three candles. The middle candle is the imbalance, the one that ran. For a bullish gap he measures from the high of the first candle's wick to the low of the third candle's wick. For a bearish gap he measures from the low of the first candle's wick to the high of the third candle's wick. The void is the part of the middle body that those two wicks do not cover. If the wicks already reach into that range, he says the void has been filled and the formation is not a gap. Overlapping wicks are evidence that resting orders were already there.

On the chart he wants a bias first. After a break of structure he treats the market as confirmed in that direction, finds the unfilled gap, and watches price trade back into it, chop, and then continue. He also shows a bearish example that retraces into a gap and keeps falling. Because the pattern is everywhere, he says a touch by itself is a poor reason to act. He wants a reaction, a candle in the expected direction, or a break of structure on a lower timeframe.

The homework is ten gaps on one chosen market, plus a hypothetical of how the chart could have been read, and also practice at rejecting the fakes. In one sketch, after a downside break, price fills a gap and closes bearish, and he draws a hypothetical stop above the prior high with earlier lows as the draw. He says the series is still assembling pieces, so that sketch is an illustration of the pattern while the method is incomplete.

## What to remember

- He uses a gap with an existing bias, as a place a retracement can react and continue. He does not use it to fade the trend.
- The lesson's measurement is wick to wick across three candles: first wick to third wick, with the middle candle as the imbalance.
- Wicks that already cover that range mean the void is gone. Those are the fakes he wants recognized.
- A gap is common enough, in his view, that the touch is not the confirmation. A reaction or a smaller-timeframe shift is.
- The stop-and-target sketch in the homework is his illustration of a pattern still being assembled, not a finished method.

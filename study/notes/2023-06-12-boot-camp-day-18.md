## What we learned

This is the recap day for fair value gaps. He says the earlier videos already covered why the market reacts to them and how to spot them. Today he puts a gap next to a trend, a liquidity sweep, and a break of structure, and he tells anyone who is fuzzy on those pieces to go back over the earlier lessons.

On the S&P he walks a small uptrend, a break of structure on the fifteen-minute, and the imbalance left behind. Neighboring wicks that overlap are not a gap in his marking. The only unfilled pocket is the one he keeps. He ignores the pre-market reaction and waits for the cash open, then scales down: the higher-timeframe imbalance is the area, a lower-timeframe break of structure is the confirmation. In the lesson's example he talks through a short whose targets sat at prior liquidity and at a small order block, with those targets reached before the remainder stopped at break-even. He is explicit that touching the gap is not the entry. Entering on the push into that pocket, in his telling of this chart, would have been stopped.

He uses the gap as a patient retracement. After a sweep and a break of structure, if the first impulse does not offer a reward he likes, he waits for price to return into the gap, drops to a lower timeframe, and looks for a break of structure that agrees with the higher-timeframe direction. On GBP/JPY he shows a fifteen-minute break and sweep, marks more than one gap, and treats a five-minute break the other way as a retrace on the higher chart, not a full trend change. On gold, a four-hour gap that price pushes clean through is dropped. A gap that lines up with resting liquidity is the one he keeps, then he looks for a lower-timeframe break after the spike.

A later bullish example uses the daily break, the accumulation that started the rally, and an hourly imbalance he prefers because a wick already filled the other supposed pocket. He points at a doji as the indecision candle from the candlestick day, and he shows price missing a first target, dipping back into the gap, and then running. He says the walkthrough is easier in hindsight and that the skill is seeing the same sequence while it is forming. His own use of the gap is mostly a map of where a retrace may go. The reason he acts is something else sitting there: an order block, a break of structure, or a sweep inside the imbalance followed by a break. The gap confirms the location more often than it is the entry by itself.

## What to remember

- A fair value gap is an unfilled pocket between wicks. Overlap removes the gap from his markup.
- The touch of the gap is the area he is watching. The action he describes waits for a lower-timeframe break of structure in the direction of the higher chart.
- A lower-timeframe break the other way can be a retrace on the higher timeframe, not a new trend.
- A gap that price runs straight through leaves his list. A gap that lines up with liquidity stays.
- In his ranking, the gap is a location and a confirmation. The sweep, the structure break, or an order block is often the reason he cares.

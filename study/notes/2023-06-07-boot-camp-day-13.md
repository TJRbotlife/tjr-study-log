## What we learned

Day 13 is his first pass at risk, and he defines it narrowly: how much is exposed, and how often. He agrees that people call risk management the important part, then he reorders the sentence. The arithmetic is easy. What is hard, he says, is the emotion around it, so he ranks discipline and a stable head above the percentage itself.

He expects early losses even after a profitable demo stretch. The jump to real money is an emotional gap, and he treats the first year or two as tuition for that gap. The job of the risk rule, in this telling, is to lose as little as possible while the skill is still forming, and to keep the original account instead of replacing it every week.

The figure he states is one to three percent of the account. He first says that per trade. He then prefers the same range as a ceiling for the whole day, so the day's plans can be one idea or a few ideas and still stay inside the ceiling. At one percent, he says, it would take a hundred straight losses to erase the account, which he treats as unrealistic. He immediately adds the catch: a hundred trades in a single day would erase it anyway. Frequency is part of the risk.

He illustrates the emotional test with his own numbers. He talks about being able to make as much as about sixty thousand dollars in a day and about losing about twenty-seven thousand dollars in one day a couple of weeks earlier, and staying flat either way. He also asks the viewer to imagine an account of about two hundred thousand dollars and to notice that twenty or thirty percent a trade, or the whole account, or ten trades a day, would feel absurd at that size. His point is to handle the current account as if it were already that large. He leaves the calculator work to the viewer: different markets convert a stop distance into a size differently, and he will not walk through it. He wants this idea in place before the series teaches entries, because the skill of reading the chart is what he says makes money possible, and size without that skill is how he says he once ended up owing people.

## What to remember

- He treats the emotion around risk as the real subject. The percentage is the easy sentence.
- Early losses are expected in his account, including after a demo that looked fine. The point of a ceiling is to keep those losses small.
- How often someone is exposed matters as much as how much, because enough trades can spend a whole account in a day.
- His large-account example is a standard of caution he wants applied while the account is still small.
- He wants the chart skill in place before size. A big result or a big loss, in his examples, is something to stay flat through.

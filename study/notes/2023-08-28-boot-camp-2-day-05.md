## What we learned

He opens with the week, because the calendar changes where he is willing to look for a bias. A pound holiday meant no volume on pound pairs, so he watched gold and the S&P. Tuesday held a stack of U.S. releases, and he was inclined to take the day, maybe looking after 10:00 if something obvious showed up. Wednesday was non-farm week: employment change and GDP an hour before the open, pending home sales half an hour into the session. He was willing to trade that day because the large prints were before the bell and could show a direction. Thursday looked similar, with unemployment claims an hour before the open, and he would look once the U.S. open hit around 9:30. Friday stacked non-farm payrolls, the unemployment rate, and manufacturing surveys through the first half hour of the session. That was too much for him, and he said he would not trade it.

The live example is gold, still open when he started and then hitting targets while he talked. Weekly structure was still bearish after a break down. The monthly had swept lows and was still bullish, which he read as a sweep that could push higher. The daily had not yet broken up, but the sweep and the immediate reaction were there. On the four-hour, accumulation gave way to a large extension, which he calls the break of the downtrend. Price then filled a fair value gap and returned into the accumulation. He scaled to the hourly, then to the session: a sweep under the lows, a break out of the chop around the weekly open, and an entry on a one-minute close out of a small fair value gap. He says the first target paid about eleven thousand dollars, and he closed more size for about 8.3 thousand dollars when the second target hit during the recording. He attributes the slow climb to the higher-time-frame lean.

He then applies the same top-down pass to the S&P. Monthly lows were swept and upside breaks followed, and he says that picture looks more developed than gold. He marks accumulation before a prior leg, and he wants a return into that area before he treats a higher objective, including monthly highs, as the draw. He says he will follow those chart levels and leave the fundamental story alone. A famous short, in his example, was early by years, and he does not have the capital to sit and guess when a collapse arrives.

The shift he describes for his own trading is to leave the hunt for direction on the lowest time frames. He wants the monthly, weekly, daily, four-hour, and hourly to say where price is reaching, then a stop with room, then the entry to arrive. On gold, accumulation, a leg up, a choppy dip, a return, and an extension were still not enough. He waited for another dip, another extension, a break of structure, and a liquidity sweep, and only then aimed at the higher-time-frame areas. He says retracements chop when they run against the side volume wants. On this gold chart, that side was up: grind down, sweep, large rally, grind again. He hopes the wider map keeps him off messy days, the same way he has used long-range reads on bitcoin, ether, and silver.

## What to remember

- A holiday, a data cluster, and a non-farm Friday each changed whether he was willing to look for a trade.
- His gold bias was a stack: weekly still down, monthly swept and still up, then a four-hour break out of accumulation.
- He marks where price can draw, including prior accumulation and monthly highs, and he will not time a fundamental collapse.
- A single extension was not the entry. He waited for the dip, the next extension, the break, and the sweep.
- Retracements, in this telling, chop when they run against the side that has the volume.
- The higher-time-frame map is what he hopes will keep him out of sessions that have nothing clean to do.

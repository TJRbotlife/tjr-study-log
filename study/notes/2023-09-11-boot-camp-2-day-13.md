## What we learned

He calls the session a red day. Three losses went out to the live room. He also took a fourth S&P long, still open and in profit when he filmed, and he kept that one to himself. Two of the called losses were on the S&P in the sense of the room's day plus his own later trade, and two were on pound/yen. He walks the stops first.

The first S&P was a long after hourly lows were swept. On the one minute, lows were swept, structure broke up, and price closed above a level he was watching. The entry he wanted was a five-minute order block created after that sweep and five-minute break. Price dipped into the zone he had marked, broke structure up on the one minute, and reacted on the five minute off the block. The stop sat under those reaction lows. Price came back and stopped it.

His own later S&P reused the same bullish read. The lows were swept again, price reacted up, and he marked the accumulation that had caused the higher move. Price broke out of that area, dipped back in, and he entered the break above it. The first target was a one-minute order block. He had already closed half. The second target was a set of highs that had not been hit. Even after the partial he was still red on the day; the second target, he says, might shrink the loss to something small. The hourly liquidity sweep that framed the long had not been invalidated. In his read, price had simply needed more time.

Pound/yen had broken a small downtrend on the hourly. On the fifteen minute he had two accumulation zones. The higher one, on the five minute, was the leg down before the leg up. The first entry was the five-minute break up by itself. It stopped almost immediately. He calls that a miss: he wanted a third confirmation, a retest, equilibrium, or a fair value gap, and he took the break alone. Price then traded into the second zone. There, the five minute broke up, price dipped into a fair value gap, reacted, broke the one minute, and rallied back above the five minute. He entered the five-minute down candle on that retest, stop under the lows. Price chopped until it stopped him. He likes this second stop more than the first, because the confluences he wanted were actually present and the chart then chopped. The small leg up is not enough, in his telling, for that higher zone to stay the one he trusts; he still wanted price in the lower accumulation.

The fourth trade is also a lesson about the room. After three called losses he would not send another idea out, even though he was willing to hold a fourth loss on his own book. He says many people in a signal room will take every call at a full size, so a fourth call would be their risk as well as his. People who do size carefully can be unhappy that a valid idea was withheld. He says he usually calls nearly every trade he takes, and this day was an exception. There was no news in the session. For the next London session he names pound news, and says a London trader should leave the pound pairs alone for it, and maybe the euro pairs as well, because that print can disturb the market.

## What we learned

This session is a short recap of one long, used to talk about size when the chart and the calendar pull in different directions. He says GDP and non-farm employment both printed in a way he read as bullish for the dollar and for the S&P, and he took that as the day's direction. On the hourly, pre-market had already broken prior highs. On the five minute he marked a zone where price had chopped before a push up, waited for a return into it, and entered after a one-minute break of structure, with the stop under that low.

In the example, the first target was a one-to-one. He closed half and moved the stop to break even. The second target was an hourly zone that had produced a large drop, a place he says resting orders could still be filled. A higher range that had already been swept stayed off the target list, because he reads that sweep as a place where shorts can fill. Both targets were hit, and the remainder stopped at break even.

The size is the point of the talk. The setup met his checks, and he still used about half his usual size. He says the day carried high-impact news inside a non-farm payrolls week, and he did not know whether the print would turn the market. A full-size loss on that kind of day is what he calls a mistake. For the next session he names unemployment claims and the PCE price index an hour before the open, and says he would use something closer to three quarters of his usual size, because the news move may already be in the price while volatility is still there.

He sorts the same idea by condition. A clean setup on a day with no news is where he may size a bit heavier. High-impact news plus a setup he still likes is a half-size day in this telling. High-impact news without a setup is a day he may skip. When the technicals feel unsure, he treats that as a further reason to stand aside, or to use a smaller size; the shaky-chart case in the talk is half of one percent in place of a one percent illustration. He says his own method is an experienced guess of lot size from confidence and probability, and he tells people watching to write a more organized plan than that so a feeling does not rewrite the size mid-trade. The one-percent case with a seven-point stop is his illustration of cutting that calculated size in half on a day like this one.

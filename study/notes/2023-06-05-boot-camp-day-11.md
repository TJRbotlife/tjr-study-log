## What we learned

Day 11 is a psychology lesson timed as the series starts to talk about execution. He says taking a loss well is the skill that has to come before entries. No one who trades consistently, in his telling, wins every time. The market is built to beat the trader, and he says growth gets easier once that is accepted.

His working picture of a handled loss is his own morning: a loss he puts at about nine thousand dollars, after which he says he did not trade again, did not increase size, and stayed inside the risk he had already chosen. He treats anger as a signal that something is already wrong: the money in the account matters too much, the risk was too large, or the trade was one of many that day. In the learning stage he wants losses on a demo account, where the event is study material. He separates that from later, when the account is large and a loss is no longer something to welcome.

The review he wants is specific. After a loss he asks why price moved against the idea, and he refuses to treat the decision as a moral failure just because it lost. The same kind of setup, he says, can lose today and win on a similar day, because conditions change and the game is probability. At the same time he rejects blaming the market or a news print. The print may have been the occasion, but the decision to be in the trade was the trader's, and the lesson is what to change.

He uses an older personal example for why risk eventually mattered to him: an account he says went from about ten thousand dollars to about one hundred twelve thousand, then was lost the next day. That episode is his stated catalyst for studying risk, not a result he offers as a target. He wants the score kept over a year, not over a day or a week. He also says there will always be another session, which is why he later wants days off around news or when the trader is already emotionally raw.

## What to remember

- He treats a perfect record as impossible for anyone who trades regularly, so a loss is expected information.
- Anger at a loss is, in this lesson, evidence that risk or trade count was already too high.
- The review he models asks why price moved that way, and it still places the decision on the trader rather than on the market or a headline.
- The same setup can win or lose on different days. He keeps the meaningful score on a year, not on one session.
- His large one-day loss is his example of why an unchecked swing forced him to study risk. It is his story, not a result to copy.

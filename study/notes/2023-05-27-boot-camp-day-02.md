## What we learned

Day 2 is the first chart lesson. He treats candlesticks as the reading layer under every later topic. A candle is one fixed slice of time. On the five-minute chart he uses, one candle is five minutes of price. A green candle closed above its open. A red candle closed below its open. The body is that open-to-close distance. The wicks are prices visited during the slice that the close did not keep.

He says a completed higher-time-frame candle can hint at the smaller chart inside it. A long wick with a weak close means price reached a level and left. A full body, with little wick against the direction of the close, is what he calls a strong close. A doji is a small body, often with long wicks, and he reads it as indecision, sometimes with a lean. The single-candle shapes he keeps are dojis, long-legged dojis, cross forms, dragonfly dojis, and hammers. He sets aside multi-candle pictures such as head-and-shoulders and double tops.

A candle is one clue. He shows a bullish-looking rejection that sits inside a sequence of lower closes, and he treats the sequence as the larger fact. Forming candles do not count. He says bias waits for the candle to finish, the same waiting he later uses for a break of structure. The assignment is to find at least ten of these closes and wicks on a familiar chart and to note how price behaved afterward.

## What to remember

- One candle is one time slice: the body is open to close, and the wicks are visits the close did not hold.
- A shape counts after that candle has closed.
- The useful shapes in this lesson are single candles. Multi-candle picture patterns are set aside.
- A bullish-looking rejection can sit inside a sequence of lower closes. The sequence still matters.
- Later lessons will use this same close-versus-wick distinction when they separate a real break from a sweep.

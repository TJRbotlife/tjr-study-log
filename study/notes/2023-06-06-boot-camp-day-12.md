## What we learned

Day 12 closes the liquidity series by showing the idea on charts and saying what it is for. He recaps the first lesson as the reason: highs and lows are where many people enter and many people get stopped, so that is where he says larger players can transact size. He recaps the second lesson as the picture: a high or low is taken, structure shifts, and direction changes. He is clear that this is one piece that can sit inside a method. It is not, in his words, the whole method.

On the daily index chart he only marks prominent highs and lows, not every wiggle. He calls any such pool a magnet. Price is drawn there because that is where he says orders can be filled. He does not treat the touch as an action. A high getting traded is not a short, and a low getting traded is not a long. He waits to see whether price reacts, and he names a break of structure plus later tools as the confirmation that the sweep actually filled orders. No reaction means he leaves it alone.

He uses the same magnet for targets. If the idea is that price will go lower after a swept high, he looks at prior prominent lows as the place where the other side can be closed, for the same reason it was opened at the high: people are entering and exiting there. His chart tour pairs a sweep with a later break, sometimes a filled imbalance or an order block, then a draw toward the opposing pool. He shows the same sequence on the four-hour and the one-hour, including a high that looks untouched until a lower timeframe reveals the sweep, and he repeats the shape on gold and on very small timeframes.

The homework changes from pure marking to pairing. He wants five sweeps, each with the price behavior that followed, so the viewer can see why he would have gained confidence. He also warns that not every high and low is a pool that filled anything. That is why he waits.

## What to remember

- He treats prominent swings as pools of orders, and he treats those pools as magnets rather than as buttons.
- A sweep earns attention only if price then reacts. Silence after the sweep is a pass in this lesson.
- The same kind of pool can be where he looks for an entry and where he looks for the move to finish.
- The pattern in his examples is not tied to one timeframe. A sweep can be invisible until the chart is dropped lower.
- Not every high or low did the work. The second sign is what he uses to separate a real pool from a mark on the chart.

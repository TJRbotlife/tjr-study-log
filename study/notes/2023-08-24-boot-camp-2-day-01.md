## What we learned

He says this season puts the day's recap and a short lesson in the same video. The lesson can be psychology or a concept he was using that session. He treats the first boot camp as already done, and he says the work here is the mind more than another pass at the basic concepts.

The index short started from a bearish four-hour and a bearish hourly before the open, so his lean was down. Price pushed into an order block and a fair value gap, then broke structure lower around a high-impact PMI release. He entered half the position on that break. He had expected the print to look strong and still send price down. Price went the other way and stopped him. His review is that he skipped a rejection off the gap or the order block, and that the higher-time-frame lean flipped while New York was open. He says that kind of loss, when the rest of the plan was intact, is one he can accept. The loss he studies harder is one taken from mixed time-frame bias because he wanted to be in a trade.

The pound-yen long was the forex sequence he uses: London lows, a sweep, then a five-minute break of structure higher. He had been avoiding pound pairs after a heavy London selloff, and he says the four-hour and hourly were not clearly with the long. He still took it. He says it met each step of that sequence, plus his read that a hard selloff often gets a relief push when the session changes. The first target was hit. He says the index loss was about thirteen thousand dollars, and the remaining pound-yen size was still open in profit when he recorded.

On the winner he still looks for a lesson. An earlier entry with the stop a few pips under the London lows would still have been stopped. The pause he respects is the quiet range after a violent London move. Once a large candle down and a reclaim showed up, he names it a Wyckoff spring out of accumulation, and he says useful ideas exist outside ICT. That breakout was extra confluence for staying in. The rest of the video is temperament. He says the method name is secondary. He knows profitable people on support and resistance, on smart-money ideas, and on a band indicator. What he tests is whether someone can hold to the stop and the target without redrawing the chart or picking up a new method while the position is open. His old habit, with one target, was to set the stop and the target and close the platforms until the next day. He also wants the recap said out loud, including the win.

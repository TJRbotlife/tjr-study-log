## What we learned

Day 15 is a written exercise in finding problems and then finding what they grow from. He says naming an issue is only the first step. If the cause is never addressed, the issue stays. The notebook page is the homework. He wants three trading problems, and he rejects strategy complaints. Not knowing a pattern yet is something the course still intends to teach. The problems he wants are about stopping, size, emotion, and psychology.

He walks a chain. The surface problem might be more trades than the plan allowed, or staying at the screen after a loss. Under that he asks why. Under the why he asks which feeling is driving it: wanting more money, anger after a loss, or fear of missing a move that will not come around again. He does not trust a second trade taken that way, especially after the first trade of the day already lost. A second example is exiting before the planned outcome, from under-confidence, confusion, a missing bias, or a gap in knowledge. Those, he says, are forms of fear.

The fixes he offers sit at the bottom of the chain, not at the top. If the fear is real money that cannot be handled, he says the trader does not know the risk, or should be on a demo, or both. If the extra trades exist because the household needs the money, he says trading is the wrong place to get it while the person is still learning, and he points at work outside the market. He is sharp about blame. The market will move as it moves. Every loss, in this lesson, is the trader's.

He thinks surface promises fail. Deciding to "just stop" after too many trades is hard because the root is still there. He wants the root removed so the same feeling cannot leak into other decisions. He uses an open profit of about twenty thousand dollars as his own example of a greedy thought, closing early to spend the money, and he says the difference is that he notices the feeling and does not act on it. He credits the method to a football coach and says he has used it outside trading as well. Once a problem is fixed, he wants it treated as something that is not repeated.

## What to remember

- The problems he wants written down are behavioral. A missing technical name is not the exercise.
- Each problem gets a chain: what happened, why it happened, which feeling drove it, and what would remove that feeling.
- He will not let a loss be filed under the market or under someone else.
- Money pressure and unhandled size are, in his examples, reasons to practice without real exposure and to get income elsewhere while learning.
- Noticing a greedy or fearful impulse, and declining to act on it, is the distinction he draws between people who share the same thoughts.

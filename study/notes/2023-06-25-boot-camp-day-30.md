## What we learned

This is the first execution lesson, and he keeps it off the charts. It is a text recap of five building blocks and the ways he stacks them into an entry. A liquidity sweep is a high or a low taken out. That only means orders might have been filled. A break of structure is the confirmation: structure has shifted, so he treats the sweep as real. In an uptrend, a sweep and then a break downward is his sign that those orders were filled and price is turning. An order block is the price range of the move that caused the sweep, the range where he says the orders sat. A fair value gap is a range that lacks opposing liquidity, usually the large candle that ran with little trade the other way. Equilibrium is the discounted side between a high and a low.

He then lists entries from the thinnest stack to the fullest. Sweep plus the structure break is the first: potential, then confirmation, and a click near what he calls the turn. If that click has poor reward, he waits for the order block, the older range where the orders were filled, and he describes the protective stop as sitting beyond the swept extreme. The pieces do not have to print at the same moment. Someone can miss the block, watch price continue, and use a fair value gap later. For a bullish idea, he reads a bullish gap as a lack of selling liquidity inside that range, and the click is the return into it or the reaction off it. The fullest stack adds equilibrium on top of the sweep, the break, and either the gap or the block, so the return is also in discount. He presents that full stack as the careful version for a beginner, and he presents the thinner combinations as the ones that carry more risk as confluence comes off.

The homework is to copy the list into the execution section of the plan from the day before, and to choose which stack belongs there. He wants the reason written in the language of orders and liquidity. He says the larger failure is knowing this click and then leaving it when emotion shows up. Learning the stack still comes first. Anyone who is fuzzy on a block is told to rewatch that block's earlier lesson before the chart days.

## What to remember

- The five blocks, in the order he recaps them, are the sweep, the structure break, the order block, the fair value gap, and equilibrium.
- A sweep is unconfirmed. The structure break is the confirmation that the sweep was real.
- The thin click is sweep plus break. The block and the gap are later locations when that first click has poor reward or was missed.
- The beginner stack he names adds the discount on top of the sweep, the break, and a block or a gap.
- This list is the execution box of the written plan from the day before.

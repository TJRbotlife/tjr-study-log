## What we learned

The lesson is his announcement of a broker change, and the reasons he gives for the choice. He says he is moving to AirFX. He walks through reviews, ownership, pricing, withdrawals, the platform, and what he still treats as unfinished.

He starts by searching the firm together with the word scam. The complaints he finds call it unregulated and offshore. His view is that a forex broker which also lists majors, minors, indexes, futures, commodities, energies, and crypto is offshore and unregulated by nature, and that U.S. forex rules make trading harder. A regulating-agency badge is not the test he uses.

He does not wave the scam reviews away. He says they likely fit the previous owners. His account is that a new group buys struggling offshore brokers, rebuilds them, and that the same group runs Hero FX. He had asked that group about Hero FX. They pointed him at AirFX and offered sharper pricing while they rebuilt it, including raw spreads and no-commission pricing, and he says he does not know how long those terms last. The check he states is whether he would place his own money there. He says he would not if he still thought it was a scam. He also says he is an early large name on the rebuilt firm, and that other large names, whom he does not name, are expected later.

On price, he says a very tight spread and a zero-commission quote are a choice. The person has to know whether they scalp, swing, or hold. He prefers paying commission if the fill lands near the price he clicked, rather than a wider fill with no commission. A deposit match, in his example of one thousand dollars matched higher, is extra trading liquidity. He says the matched amount cannot simply be withdrawn, the person's own cash is still the original deposit, and he will not use the program. He might see a use for someone who cannot otherwise place a very small index trade. He also wants instant fills.

Withdrawals are part of the choice. He repeats a claim of about fifteen minutes and says he has not tested it. People he knows at Hero FX described something similar. He contrasts Hanko Trade, where a withdrawal took about a day and a half and the crypto address had to be confirmed again before the transfer. The platform reason is MetaTrader 5. Orders can be placed from the chart, and a stop can be dragged to the entry, or a target dragged to a new price, without looking up the number. He says MetaTrader 4 was usable and that he still prefers the newer platform. He likes majors, minors, exotics, energies, indexes, futures, metals, stocks, and crypto on one login. He still keeps longer investments off the trading balance. When he recorded, deposits were crypto, and he described wire, bank, and card processing as still on the way. Hedging and automated tools are on the list of what the firm allows. He says a practice login exists, and he wants that used until the person is already profitable.

## What to remember

- He chose by reading the scam reviews, then separating a regulation complaint from a complaint about who holds the money.
- The test he states is his own funds on the platform, and a regulator badge is not that test.
- Spread versus commission, in this lesson, depends on whether the style needs the fill or the cheaper quote.
- A deposit match, as he describes it, is margin to trade with, and the matched sum stays locked.
- Withdrawal speed was still an untested claim, set against Hanko Trade taking about a day and a half.
- MetaTrader 5's chart orders, and a practice login before live size, are part of the choice he described.

## What we learned

Part two puts the first two execution stacks on a chart. He says there are several ways to assemble the blocks, and that each person still has to choose how to join them. He frames the series as the bare strategy. Other filters, including the daily direction, sit around it. The stacks by themselves, he says, do not produce profitability. They are the click. Time, and the later lessons, are what he thinks shorten the losing stretches that come from one repeated mistake.

The first pattern is a liquidity sweep and a break of structure. He walks cases where resting liquidity sits beyond a high or a low. A sweep can run through it and still fail to qualify, until structure actually breaks. After the break, the charts he shows draw toward older pools of liquidity. He calls this version the simplest and the lowest in confluence, which makes it the riskiest of the stacks. The lesson is only the location of the click. Stops and profit-taking sit outside what he is showing, though he will point at an imbalance or a prior high as the kind of draw the blocks already describe. He warns against treating the examples as a reason to take a large number of trades. The homework for this half is ten examples of the sweep and the break, then a look at what price did afterward, so the pattern builds confidence before anyone uses it.

The second pattern adds the order block. After the sweep and the break, he finds the leg that caused the sweep, the candle where he says the orders were filled, and the click is the return into that range. In several examples the structure-break candle itself is a worse location than the later touch of the block. He shows the same sequence on higher timeframes and on small ones, and he changes asset as he goes. He says the repetition should become something a person can call without stopping to think. The homework is another ten, on one or two markets the viewer already studies, continued until the sequence is automatic. Later topics about the daily direction and about targets, he says, sit on top of this recognition.

## What we learned

Day 45 says the slowdown comes from adding pieces, not from a hole in the method. The trading plan is there so the session already has a count, a time, a reason, and an execution path. If that plan is followed, the remaining miss is the market doing something else, plus the time it takes to learn how price tends to move. He says a week of losses gets misread as a broken strategy. The strategy in the lessons is the one he uses. The gap he names is screen time: reading price, and having seen enough opens to know where the day wants to go.

He splits the skill. Finding the day's direction is the part he calls straightforward once the earlier lessons are in place. The harder part is the execution area and the quality of the trade around it. Details like a late-session order block in thin volume are the kind of thing he says a loss teaches. They are not a reason to bolt on another method. He puts a long wait on the calendar: someone who actually stays with one plan may need on the order of a year before the experience is there, and many people take longer. Different quarters and sessions are part of why that wait exists.

The complication he wants removed is a stack of methods in the same head. He says he used to carry about twenty strategies at once and read the market through all of them. The replacement is one strategy and one bias, built by the trader. Watching someone else long enough to see why they got a bias is fine. Keeping that person's bias after the skill is there is the dependency he wants dropped. His own earlier example is a signal room that called the opposite of his read. He took the room's side and lost. Sometimes the room was wrong and the independent read was right. He says there is no hidden extra component. The missing piece is time in the market.

Once the four building blocks and the daily bias are known, he wants expertise in those, not a fifth concept. Adding a tool means another skill that has to be learned before the results can change, which pushes the timeline out. His older forex work, the session split built on thirty-minute boxes, is the illustration: he did not keep adding to it. He traded it until he understood the market's behavior around it. The same idea applies to a skipped trade. He wants the time frames written down, then a look at where price actually drew, so the next time that alignment shows up the earlier note is already there. He says he wrote that way for about two years. The public recaps are what that journal became. Marking every sweep and break and writing what price did next is the study he says is left.

## What to remember

- The plan is the constraint: one count, one window, one reason, one execution path. A dull week is read here as missing experience, not as a broken method.
- One bias, built from the chart, replaces a stack of strategies and a borrowed daily idea. Someone else's read is a temporary way to see the reasoning.
- He treats time in the market as the piece people keep trying to replace with a new tool. A year of staying with one plan is the scale he gives for a typical learner.
- Extra building blocks add skills that also have to be learned. The four already named, plus the daily bias, are what he wants known deeply.
- A skipped idea written down, then checked against where price drew, is how the same alignment is supposed to be recognized the next time it appears.

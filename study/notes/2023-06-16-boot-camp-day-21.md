## What we learned

The assigned topic is how to take a win, and he says it matters more than how to take a loss. A green result is not proof that everything was done well. Skipping the review, because the trade paid, is the mistake. A win can be accidental, and the confidence that follows is how he describes the slide into greed, overconfidence, and taking more than the plan allowed.

He wants a win handled with the same flatness as a loss. His own large swings, including a heavy loss the morning of the video, are examples of why he thinks in percentages and probabilities rather than in the cash figure on the screen. He walks a hypothetical large account to show the trap: a planned fraction of the account becomes a dollar amount that feels too big, and people close before the target or the stop has done its job, acting on the money. He says he has had to leave a large open result alone because the plan said so.

After early wins he fell into taking another trade and risking more because the last one worked. His counter is one trade, then done, whether it paid or not. Happiness about a win will show up. Acting on it is the problem he names: announcing it, or screenshotting a trade that is still running. He says those screenshots often mark the high, and then the trade comes back. Until there is a real record of consistency, a single win is not evidence of skill, and posting it while the record is still unproven is something he calls out.

The review is the same job as the review after a loss, and he thinks it is rarer, so it is more worth keeping. He wants the journal to say why the win happened: what lined up, what was repeated on purpose, what should happen again. A win is not a day off. The hunger he wants is to repeat the process, which he separates from the urge to trade more that same day. On the loss side, he wants the mistake written down so it is not repeated. Day-to-day cash is the wrong score. He points at the month and the year, and at the win rate, and he suggests watching the chart while the position is on and leaving the account balance alone until those longer marks.

The second half leaves the trade review. He frames the series as a free record of what trading did for his life, and he says people who are already doing well have, in his experience, tried to pull others up with them. Gatekeeping and assuming public results are fake are the habits he associates with staying stuck. Giving, for him, beats receiving. He tells a Father's Day story about a watch for his dad, and he says a gift to someone who was struggling moved him more than anything he has been given.

He then talks about the years before the public version of him. He describes himself as a middle child who built the skill alone, a long stretch of bad mental health, and a public smile that hid it. He says he tried to end his life more than once, and that the point of saying so is that the viewer in that same place is not on their own. Trading, in this telling, is a self-improvement path, and the money is not the whole of it. People who only see the wins did not see him at the bottom. Criticism, he says, does not land the way it once would. He would rather be seen taking hits and getting back up than satisfied with a life he hates. He says trading is what freed him, and that the work he actually cares about now is helping. He closes the hour by returning to the win: learn how to take one, and take the rest of the video as part of the same lesson.

## What to remember

- A paid trade still gets a review. The question is why it worked, and whether that sequence can be repeated.
- Acting on the high, including another trade or a larger risk because the last one paid, is the failure mode he describes.
- The score he cares about is the month, the year, and the percentage, not the feeling of one green day.
- The later half of the video is the claim that the skill is also a way out of a bad stretch, and that he is speaking from having lived one.
- Giving and staying available to people who are struggling are the uses he gives the result, once the result exists.

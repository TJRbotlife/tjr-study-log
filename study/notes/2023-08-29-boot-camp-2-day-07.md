## What we learned

He starts with the index trade from the prior update. The second and third targets were hit. He still expects a fourth. The body of the lesson is the pound-yen trade that lost.

The setup was the same accumulation idea. Price built a base, broke out, and he wanted a return to the prices where orders were filled before that leg. A liquidity sweep and a break of structure higher were in place. All he wanted next was a revisit of that area and a reaction off it. Price swept some lows, reacted, dipped again, and he entered on a later candle rather than the first reaction. He says a fuller account of accumulation, distribution, and a change of trend can wait for another video. On this chart he is using the same concepts.

The stop sat under the five-minute low. He says the reward was not especially clean, and he still liked the path because the hourly was an uptrend. He had described this kind of day before: the market can change direction after the steps he cares about are done. He lists them here. Price came into the accumulation, or the order block. It broke structure higher. It returned. It reacted, and he waited two more candles for that reaction. Targets sat at highs that would have continued the trend. Price dumped, broke the uptrend, and flipped the bias. That, he says, is the loss he wants. The steps were done, and the market changed its mind.

He says the loss was about eight thousand dollars. After it, runners on the index still left him up about eight thousand dollars on the day, with more open if the next index target hit, and he was also up a further amount on the index while he recorded. He is pleased with the index since the day before, and with gold. Gold had reached three targets and then stopped back at break-even, and he says the higher-time-frame lean stayed intact. He still wanted gold higher. On the four-hour, price later revisited the range that had fueled the first leg, swept liquidity, broke structure on the five-minute, and rallied, which he reads as staying with a monthly lean he would have been willing to hold for a long climb. He calls the pound-yen loss one of the better ones in a while because the index runners covered it and the risk on that one trade stayed contained.

## What to remember

- The loss he is willing to praise is one where the return, the break, the reaction, and the hourly trend were all in place.
- A clean reward multiple was not what made this pound-yen idea acceptable to him.
- He skipped the first reaction candle and waited for a later one, and the trade still lost when the trend broke.
- About eight thousand dollars on that trade was, in his telling, contained because earlier index runners covered it.
- A stop back at break-even on gold did not, for him, cancel the higher-time-frame lean.
- He is happy with a loss when the steps he had named were actually taken.

## What we learned

Day 10 is where to find liquidity, after the prior lesson on what it is. He recaps his premise: large players need the other side of the market before they can fill size and then push price. Liquidity, in this telling, is the crowd willing to be that other side.

He locates that crowd at obvious trend points. In an uptrend he says retail traders treat a new high as proof the trend continues, so buy stops sit just above prior highs. At the same prices, he says, sit the stops of people who tried to short the pullback and who are wrong if that high breaks. One print above the high therefore takes people out and puts other people in. He says that pile is what lets larger players fill and then turn the market, often back down. A downtrend is the same picture inverted: sell stops under lows, plus the stops of people who tried to buy the bounce.

He ties this to an earlier trend lesson. Trends do not last forever, so a sweep of the obvious extreme is how he says a trader can catch the start of the next move rather than the middle of the old one. He walks the idea on many timeframes, from daily and monthly index charts down through intraday, and he says the same sweep-then-reverse shows up on all of them. He mentions an order block only as a later name for the zone that gets filled after the sweep.

He is explicit that spotting this is not yet a trade. He says liquidity plus a break of structure is not enough for a bias. The homework is chart study: five sweeps on each of three timeframes, on whatever market the viewer chooses.

## What to remember

- He puts the crowd above highs in an uptrend and under lows in a downtrend: continuation orders on one side, protective stops on the other.
- A sweep, in his account, can clear both groups at once and leave room for a turn.
- He uses that turn to look for the start of a new trend, because he does not treat the prior trend as permanent.
- The same sweep shows up in his examples from monthly charts down to intraday charts.
- Marking the sweep is the skill this lesson asks for. He withholds a trade until more of the method is in place.

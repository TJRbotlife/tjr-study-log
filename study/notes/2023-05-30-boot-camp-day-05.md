## What we learned

Day 5 is the second mindset lesson, on a method of goal setting he says he learned in a basketball program. The failure he describes is a distant aim with no path, the same shape as a New Year's resolution that never starts. He wants three long-term end goals, on the order of years, and at least one of them may be trading-related. The other two can be ordinary life aims. He also wants people to admit they do not yet know the route.

Under each end goal he asks for ten shorter goals or steps, thirty in all. Some of those steps are daily acts, such as following a plan, keeping risk consistent, and journaling. Others are nearer milestones, such as a profitable week and then a profitable month. Climbing them, he says, is the only route he believes in: small daily gains, with the path written down in advance.

The wording matters as much as the list. Each line is written as "I am" or "I will," and it is read aloud at least five times a day as if the result is already true. He says it is hard to speak a sentence you do not hold, so the repetition is meant to force belief and behavior together. His own practice was to keep the list at the back of a notebook and to rewrite all three aims and their steps every morning. Embarrassment at saying the sentences in front of other people is treated as a sign the aim is not really held. The same notebook is the one he wants kept for the journal the series will teach.

## What to remember

- Three distant end goals, with ten steps written under each.
- The lines are phrased "I am" or "I will," and they are spoken aloud each day.
- A distant aim with no steps stays a wish. The steps are the path.
- Rewriting the list each morning was his way of keeping the path in view.
- The notebook that holds the goals is the same notebook he wants used when journaling is taught.

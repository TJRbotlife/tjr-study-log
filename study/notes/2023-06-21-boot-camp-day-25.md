## What we learned

Day 25 is a psychology talk set just before equilibrium. He says the tools are still incomplete, so the subject is the headspace around a winning day. The occasion is a session in which every target in his recap was hit. He says a beginner in that spot would go straight back to the chart and try to turn the gain into a larger one. He calls that overconfidence, and he puts it in the same family as greed.

He frames the activity as probability. In the way he trades, the open is the strong window, because that is when new orders arrive, a liquidity sweep shows up, and those orders have to be filled before price can run. After that, he says volume fades, the later entries are retracements, and the reward thins out. That is why he keeps a day to one trade, or two at most, and treats one as the better version.

The same mood shows up as a rescue trade. He says searching for one position large enough to climb out of a hole forces a bias onto a market that is not prepared for it, usually late, usually with size that does not match the odds. The correction he repeats is that the person watching is still learning, and that "profitable" is a label earned over a long stretch. Holding that fact, he says, removes the urge to size up. A fresh video title can create the same mood. In the case he picks apart, the newly named model was still a fair value gap, and the confidence came from the new label.

He draws a narrow band of confidence. Enough to take the trade that fits. Past that band is extra size, extra trades, and a belief in a perfect win rate. Short of that band is fear: a missed entry, a late click, too little size. In his picture, a loss after an oversized win throws someone from the top of the band to the bottom. On his own win streaks he says he already expects a loss, because the streak only shows that he is still human. A short beginner streak has not shown a long run of profitability. If that run does arrive, he says the habits that produced it, including the risk limit and the low trade count, are the part to keep. His own warning story is a profitable practice account, then a live account where he raised leverage because the practice results felt like proof.

He closes on news. He says he stands aside when a Federal Reserve chair is speaking, and he tells people who are still unprofitable in quiet conditions to leave that day and the day after alone. A lucky win on that kind of session, he says, is the result that convinces someone to size up, and the next speech is where that confidence gets tested.

## What to remember

- Overconfidence, in this lesson, is the urge to trade again because the last result worked.
- He treats the open as the stronger window and later session trades as lower quality.
- Fear and overconfidence sit on either side of the confidence he wants: able to click, unwilling to inflate size or count.
- A win streak is, in his account, a reason to expect a loss and to keep the habits that were already working.
- A new name on a fair value gap was his example of confidence that arrived with a video title.

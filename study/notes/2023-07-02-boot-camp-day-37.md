## What we learned

He places take-profit levels on the same building blocks used for entries: order blocks, fair value gaps, and draws on liquidity. Those are the places he expects price to react or to fill orders, so he treats them as magnets.

The frame of the target follows the frame of the execution, or one frame higher. A short idea on the five-minute looks first to the fifteen-minute for the nearest draw in the trade's direction, then to other blocks beyond it. He often talks through three or four targets and says the count itself is not the point. A fifteen-minute entry taken in service of a four-hour idea does not, for him, force the first target out to four-hour liquidity. If the trade is entered on a low frame, the first magnets stay on that frame or the next one up.

He ranks this skill under daily bias, then execution, then the stop. If the entry was well placed, he expects price to move, and he says the first target in his examples is often near one-to-one rather than a distant multiple. In the four-hour example he lines that first target up with a high that matches one-to-one, then adds further draws and an order block. On a foreign-exchange chart he stacks a gap, a four-hour order block, an hourly order block, and a more distant liquidity pool.

A Friday trade he closed around breakeven is used only to show placement. Entry came from a five-minute break and a one-minute block. The first target was a five-minute order block, because he does not want one-minute confluences setting the target when a higher frame is available. Further targets were the next liquidity pool and a fifteen-minute gap. One short he draws on an uptrend is labeled, by him, as a bad trade shown only so the targets are visible.

## What to remember

- Targets are the same building blocks used for entries, placed in the direction of the bias.
- The first target belongs on the execution frame or one frame above it, even when the idea comes from a higher chart.
- He treats one-minute levels as too small to set the target when a five-minute block is available.
- He places this skill after bias, execution, and the stop.
- A chart drawn on an uptrend is, in his words, a bad short. It is only a drawing of where targets would sit.

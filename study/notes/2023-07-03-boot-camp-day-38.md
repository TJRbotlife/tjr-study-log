## What we learned

The stop is an invalidation, not a fixed count of points. After a liquidity sweep, a break of structure, and an entry, he puts the stop beyond the sweep. If price trades back through that high, he says the sweep did not take liquidity the way the idea required, and the orders he thought were filled did not push price with the bias. The same line is used when the entry itself is a fair value gap or an order block. A stop tucked just beyond the entry candle is too close, because price can lift slightly and still go his way.

He describes an exception he does not like. When the sweep is so far away that the distance to target looks poor, he has put the stop beyond a nearer order block or gap. The true invalidation, he says, is still the sweep, so that tighter stop can take him out while the bias remains intact.

The day's trade is the worked example. It was stopped, then price continued with the broader bullish bias. He treats that as the specific execution being wrong. He had called a five-minute sweep and a one-minute break the setup, and price traded through that sweep. The daily bias can still be right after an execution stop is hit. He also wants the stop a little past the level, not on the exact print, because the spread can stop the trade before the level itself breaks. On the example he talks about about half a point of room, and he says the buffer should match that pair's spread.

When a fifteen-minute sweep sits too far for a comfortable distance to the target, he says the alternative is a lower frame, still under the sweep if someone wants the extra room, and still inside whatever distance that person can tolerate. He also says he had reduced his own size that day because of high-impact news and a bank holiday the next day. Those figures stay his. At the close he says a U.S. bank holiday slows the markets, and he does not want that session traded.

## What to remember

- The stop marks the price that proves that particular sweep or block was not valid.
- An execution can be invalidated while the daily bias is still intact. He uses the day's stopped trade to say so.
- A stop on the exact print can be hit by the spread. He wants a small buffer past the level, matched to the pair.
- He is uneasy with a tighter stop that is not the real invalidation, even when the distance to target looks better.
- News and a holiday are why he describes reducing his own size and sitting out the holiday session. The size itself stays his example.

## What we learned

He recaps three trades and uses them to show a stop that does not kill the idea, as long as the bias is still intact and some of the day's risk is still unused. Two were on the S&P and one was on pound/dollar. The S&P had broken a downtrend. He was looking for the accumulation that caused the push up. Pre-market tapped it once, and the cash open pushed deeper into the one-minute zone that had started that leg. He marked a fair value gap, entered as soon as the bottom of it filled, and used a five-point stop. He calls the entry antsy. In hindsight he wanted a reaction off the gap, and a stop under the level he treats as invalidation. Either change, he says, could have kept him out of that stop.

The theme is the re-entry. He had only used half of what he was willing to lose that day, because pound/dollar was also setting up. After the stop he noticed the sweep low had never been taken, so the bullish read was still standing. Price broke structure up again, filled a fair value gap, and reacted, which he counts as another layer of confluence. He re-entered with the stop under that invalidation. He took half off at the first target and half of what was left at the second, then closed the remainder when the one-minute broke down off higher-time-frame liquidity. Part of that close was simply that he did not want to sit in the trade any longer. He says the close still looked right.

Pound/dollar followed the same sequence. A downtrend broke, and he marked the accumulation that caused the change of direction. The third confirmation he wanted on the five minute — a fair value gap, equilibrium, or an order block — did not show on the first pullback. Price swept the lows again, broke five-minute structure up, and he then marked the small accumulation that caused that break. A one-minute break and a five-minute reaction candle were the entry. The first target hit, half came off, and the stop moved to break even, with further targets up at a higher-time-frame accumulation area. He also notes a pre-market and New York break of a larger downtrend as a reason the long was allowed.

The risk arithmetic is the lesson he wants remembered. The first S&P stop had used about half of the day's allowance. After the re-entry's first target he was, in this telling, down to about a quarter of that allowance and still about five thousand dollars down on the day. That leftover room is what let him put about three quarters of the day's risk on the pound idea. If that one stopped, the day would have used the full allowance; if the S&P's second target hit, the book was already green and the S&P stop was at break even. Using the whole day's risk on the first, jumpy entry would have left no room for either later trade. He separates that first fill as an avoidable mistake from the later two, which he says waited for the points he wanted. On the pound example he states a reward of about 1 to 5.8 if every marked target were hit.

## What to remember

- The first S&P stop came from an immediate gap fill and a stop that sat above the level he later called invalidation.
- He treated the bullish read as still alive because the sweep low had not been taken.
- The re-entry in the example used that standing bias, another break, and a reaction, then closed the rest on a one-minute break down.
- After the first target on the re-entry, the lesson's book was still red, which is the room he used for the later pound trade.
- A full-size first loss, in his telling, would have blocked both later entries.
- He separated the jumpy first fill from the later trades, which he said waited for the confirmations he wanted.

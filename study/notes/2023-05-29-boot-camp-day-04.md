## What we learned

Day 4 defines a trend as momentum. Once price is printing a sequence, he expects that sequence to continue until it breaks. An uptrend is higher highs and higher lows. A downtrend is lower highs and lower lows. The third state is consolidation: price chops, and the higher-high or lower-low sequence is absent until a swing is actually broken and a new sequence begins.

The higher time frame outranks the lower one. He says a chart can show many direction changes on a 15-minute view inside one weekly or four-hour trend, and that the larger trend is the one that states where price is trying to go. The smaller chart is for seeing that larger move more closely. A sweep or a structure shift on a low time frame is useful to him only when it agrees with the daily or four-hour sequence. He names trading against that higher-frame direction as one of his own recurring mistakes, including a losing stretch he traces to ignoring the higher chart.

Inside a trend, the legs that extend the sequence are the ones that make new extremes. The legs back against the trend are the smaller retracements, and he says their depth is unpredictable from the trend alone. A line chart is offered as a way to see the sequence when candle color is distracting. The assignment is a written forecast, on one or two markets, of where the week seems headed, then the next few days, then the day. He is explicit that this forecast is not yet a position.

## What to remember

- An uptrend prints higher highs and higher lows. A downtrend prints lower highs and lower lows. Consolidation is the state with no such sequence.
- The higher time frame's sequence outranks a swing that only appears on a smaller chart.
- Counter-moves inside a trend are the smaller legs. The legs that extend the trend are the ones that make new extremes.
- A line chart is a reading aid when candle color hides the sequence.
- A forecast of direction, in this lesson, is a study note. He separates it from building a position.

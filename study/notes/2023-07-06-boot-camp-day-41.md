## What we learned

Day 41 is about a stretch of losses, not a new model. He says he has lost the past two sessions of the week, and he treats that as a reason to look for a small adjustment. He keeps the method. In his words, a method that has already worked for years is not thrown out, and it is not something that suddenly got patched. The useful question is which small piece is producing the same bad result.

He splits the adjustment into two cases. Sometimes the market itself is trading poorly, so the small change is the market. He gives his own example from about two months earlier: the S&P was not trading well, he went back to forex, a market he already knew, and he says that stretch included a week he puts at about one hundred thousand dollars. He presents that figure as his own result from a switch he already knew how to make. In the current stretch he says price action is fine and the daily idea is still being fulfilled. The miss is the entry. He had been entering on a low-time-frame liquidity sweep and a break of structure alone. Price would stop him out and then go the way he had called. He says the better samples in his journal added an order block, a fair value gap, or equilibrium on top of that sweep and break.

He wants the journal to make that pattern obvious: the same mistake across many losses, then one piece trimmed. Two losing trades are, for him, ordinary, and he still wants them reviewed. He reserves the phrase losing streak for something closer to two or three losing weeks, and he says the response is the same review. On wins he asks the same questions: why it worked, and how the reward relative to the risk could have been cleaner.

He treats trying to earn the loss back immediately as the move that makes the stretch worse. He says he can wait, because he has already seen the method pay over later weeks and months. He also wants the bias updated when a higher-time-frame break of structure goes against it. A bullish idea held through a bearish higher-time-frame break is, in this lesson, how stops pile up. The market was going to move that way regardless. He places the loss on the person who pressed the button, and he says the hours on the chart plus the written review are how the next version of the same situation gets cleaner.

## What to remember

- A red stretch, in this lesson, is a pattern hunt inside a method he already trusts. The method stays; one repeated miss is the candidate for a tweak.
- His two cases are a market that is no longer behaving, and an entry that is too thin even when the bias is right. The recent chart examples were the second case.
- The journal is how he sees the same sweep-and-break stop repeating, and how he decides an order block, a fair value gap, or equilibrium belongs on the next attempt.
- Trying to earn a loss back in the same stretch is the behavior he says makes the damage worse. Two red trades are not what he calls a streak.
- A higher-time-frame break against the idea is information about the bias. He puts the loss on the execution, and he reviews wins with the same questions.

## What we learned

He teaches daily bias again, this time before the open, with the charts on screen. Weekly direction comes first. He wants the extension of the daily trend, not the smaller retrace against it, because the legs in the trend direction travel farther.

On the S&P he is openly uncomfortable with the upside. Monthly structure is breaking up, he does not like the fundamentals, and he also says that discomfort is not a bias. The daily had closed back above and broken structure upward, so the day is bullish until the chart says otherwise. He describes the prior session's short as a daily liquidity sweep taken without lower-timeframe confirmation, and he calls that the miss.

The second example keeps weekly and daily apart. The weekly is up and sitting near a weekly order block after a liquidity sweep, but the daily is still bearish. Until the daily flips, he stays short-biased and looks for a fair value gap, an order block, and a sweep, then a fifteen-minute break and a five-minute break for a tighter entry. Gold is simpler in the lesson: weekly and daily are both down, price is retracing, and he marks equilibrium, discount, and a gap. He waits for those prices to be swept or filled before using the same blocks on a lower chart.

On the S&P the hourly, four-hour, daily, weekly, and fifteen-minute are all up, so he marks several long zones and says the location price actually fills matters less than adapting inside the bias. The short on the other pair is different. The daily is down, but the hourly and four-hour are still up, so he wants at least a fifteen-minute break of structure before he treats that short as ready. He also says to stay out of the window right after the news print that morning.

## What to remember

- The day's direction is daily structure, not the fundamental story he prefers.
- He wants the trend's extension. The counter-leg is the smaller move.
- A daily liquidity sweep is not enough if the lower frames never confirm. He names that as the prior session's error.
- Weekly and daily can disagree. He trades the daily until it flips, and he still keeps the weekly as the larger map.
- Aligned frames mean several zones in that direction. A daily idea against a still-bullish hourly and four-hour needs a lower-frame break first.

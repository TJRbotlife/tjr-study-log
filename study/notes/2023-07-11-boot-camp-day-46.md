## What we learned

Day 46 is the journal layout he says people asked for after the previous talk. He wants every factor that could have changed the decision, compared with looking at why one post traveled and another did not. The page is columns, then sentences. The short fields are the pair, the session, and the risk. The long fields are the confluences, whether the plan was followed, the emotion and its cause, and what could have been better.

He wants the pair written because the market itself is information, and he tells someone who is still learning to keep a single name on the watchlist until that name is understood. The session is New York or London in his own book. Asian hours are the stretch he skips, and his picture of them is waiting about an hour for a move of about five pips. He says that time can be spent on something useful instead. The risk column is where the percent used on that trade is written. In this lesson he says his own preference is about 1 percent, that a band of about 1 to 3 percent is what he accepts, and that more than that is already a mistake before the chart is judged. A smaller figure is fine with him. He also accepts a size that was calculated the way the earlier lot-size lesson described, as long as it is calculated every time.

Confluences are written in sentences: what the daily, four-hour, hourly, and fifteen-minute each showed, and which building blocks the entry used. A liquidity sweep and a break of structure might be the whole entry, or those two plus a fair value gap or an order block. The point of rereading the week is a repeated pattern, such as buys taken while the daily was bearish and only the hourly agreed. The plan field is a yes with the steps that were actually followed, or a list of the steps that were skipped. He expects a skipped plan to line up with the loss, and he wants that sentence to function as the reminder. Emotions are named and explained: fear of entering, overconfidence on a loser, greed that kept moving a target, cutting a trade before the stop, a stop in the wrong place, or a profit area at a building block that was never marked. The cause matters. Price running in the trade's direction is his example of why greed shows up, and the journal is supposed to connect that feeling to taking the profit at a level that can reverse.

Wins get the same improvement pass, which he says is harder and more useful. Losses are the obvious version: the entry needed more confirmation, the daily bias was ignored because of anxiety to be in, the risk was too large, or the exit ignored the stop. He ties the page to his own recent week. Trades taken on a sweep and a break alone were the losing set. Waiting for an order block or a fair value gap is what he says changed the next sessions, including the S&P position he is watching while he talks. Saying the lesson in one's head is not the journal. The sentence has to exist on the page. He also says time alone will not produce the skill if the sessions are only repeated without that review. Effort that stays casual, in this lesson, stays casual in the results.

## What to remember

- The journal he describes is a record of pair, session, risk used, every confluence including the higher time frames, plan-following, emotion plus cause, and one improvement on both wins and losses.
- A repeated line across the week is the point of rereading it. The lesson's example is buys against a bearish daily.
- His risk band in this video is his own plan's number for that column. The note stores it as what he says he writes down.
- A feeling such as greed or an early exit belongs in the same entry as the chart reason, so the next similar session can be recognized.
- He treats casual repetition without the page as the case where time does not produce skill.

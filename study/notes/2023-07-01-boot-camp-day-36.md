## What we learned

He uses the weekly to interpret a daily break. A daily break down inside a weekly uptrend is, for him, a weekly retrace, and he says the higher frame holds more power at every step of the chart. Retracement tools, at least equilibrium and a fair value gap, are how he estimates where that retrace might pause. He had expected a close under a monthly low. Price did not give it. He treats that as acceptable and returns to structure rather than arguing with the candle.

Once daily structure has broken up, he wants buys, then he checks the four-hour and the hourly for the same direction. High-confluence zones are marked on those frames. Only when price arrives does he look for a sweep and a break on the fifteen-minute or five-minute. A break the other way on a small frame is the retrace of the frame above it. A fifteen-minute break down can be an hourly retrace, and a one-minute break is not a new trend. The aim is the extension that agrees with the higher frame, not the short opposing leg.

If a marked zone is touched and does not react, he drops it, as if it had never been marked. On the pair with a daily downtrend and a weekly uptrend, he still looks for shorts, but not for a run that would finish the weekly move, because the weekly is not yet in a discount. Discount, an order block from the impulse, and nearby liquidity are the draws. A four-hour break up, in that setting, is the daily retrace. Once price is in a discount and into a four-hour or hourly order block, a reaction on the fifteen-minute and the five-minute is what he describes using for the prior session's short. He stays with the daily downtrend until the daily breaks back up, which is how he would say the weekly retrace is finished.

Gold in the lesson is down on both the weekly and the daily. A four-hour break up is again a daily retrace. He waits for a fifteen-minute break down before any execution, and he lists daily liquidity and possible order blocks as draws sitting in the background. On that chart he shows stacked targets, including a lesson example he puts at about one to 4.8 on the first daily draw and about one to 13.5 if held the whole way. He says he would take partials and would not hold the full distance.

## What to remember

- The frame above explains the frame below. A break against the higher trend is a retrace until that higher structure flips.
- Small-frame breaks do not reset the daily bias.
- A zone that does not react is removed from the map.
- When daily and weekly disagree, the daily sets the session direction and the weekly limits how far he expects that session to run.
- Far daily targets in the lesson's chart example stay optional. He describes partials rather than holding the whole distance.

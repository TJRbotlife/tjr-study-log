## What we learned

He uses this day to close the order-block series by doing the spotting homework in public. The definition is unchanged. An order block is the leg up or the leg down that comes just before the break of structure. That leg is the move that causes the liquidity sweep, so it is the price range where the orders were filled. The break is the structure shift. When price returns, the same range can fill more orders, because the first pass already showed that people were willing to transact there, and then price can continue with the new direction.

The new detail is how he draws the box. The version he recommends while the pattern is still new is the entire leg. He also shows his own tighter habit: from the wick to where the body starts. He says price often reacts at a tap of the wick, or at the base of the body, and he calls that a thing he has seen often rather than a rule he can justify. He does not want a beginner copying the tight box. His comparison is a player who cannot make a layup and is already shooting from distance. Mark the whole leg first.

He also uses a block as a target, not only as a place to look for a return. On the charts he strings a sweep, a break, a return into a gap or a block, and a later target at prior liquidity or at another block. The gold example is the one he offers as live reading. A sweep and a rally have already broken structure, and price is coming back. The retracement tools he is willing to use at this point in the series are the fair value gap and the order block. Equilibrium is named only as something he has not covered, so it is not in the example. He waits for a break of structure as confirmation. A tap of the higher-timeframe block by itself is not enough. Bullish daily and four-hour structure is why he is looking for bullish confirmation on the way back, and he still wants the lower-timeframe break before the idea is complete.

When the range is huge, he says there may be little to do with it until a lower timeframe makes it specific. If price is already inside the leg, he will wait for a small further push and a return, or for an imbalance inside the range. The pattern he wants recognized on sight is simple: a liquidity sweep and a leg away from it imply a block at that leg. Seeing it cleanly, he says, is practice and time in front of the market, not another definition.

## What to remember

- The leg before the break is still the definition: sweep, filled orders, structure shift, possible return.
- The beginner markup is the whole leg. The wick-to-body box is his personal habit, earned from seeing taps, and he does not want it copied early.
- A block can be a target as well as a place to watch for a return.
- A tap without a lower-timeframe break of structure is incomplete in the examples he walks.
- A very wide leg is a reason to drop down a timeframe or to wait, not a reason to force a precise box.

## What we learned

He is late for a check-in he promised after two weeks. The series is in its third week, and the first check-in is the one that actually told him what people wanted next. This video is that ask again. He wants comments that name a topic, a small practical question, or something in the person's actual life. Praise by itself has become common enough that it no longer tells him what to teach.

He treats the boot camp as a two-way conversation. A paid course that cannot hear the student is the comparison he rejects. Because a video goes up every day, a comment can become the next explanation. He already has a path through the main building blocks and the psychology talks. After that, the material he wants is the pile of small pieces. He says five years in the market is mostly those pieces, and that becoming consistent is a long run of losses that each teach one of them. His example of a small piece is waiting after a news print for price to develop, instead of acting on the release itself.

He is already collecting requests in a notes app. The ones he names are how to close part of a position, where a stop belongs, and where to take profit. He says the take-profit question has shown up more than once. The deal he describes is that the audience is the reason he can do the work, so questions get answered rather than stored as fan mail.

The other half of the check-in is how he wants to be spoken to. The video is one person on each side of the screen, even when many people are watching. He does not want to be handled as an idol. A normal greeting, a real question, and ordinary life are the comments he says he will use. The same idea covers meeting him in public. He tells a gym story from Puerto Rico in which the conversation went well because he was treated as an ordinary person, and he contrasts that with people at a conference who circled him and only approached once they had worked up the nerve. He would rather the conversation start as if they already knew each other.

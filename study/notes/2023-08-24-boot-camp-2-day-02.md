## What we learned

The gold trade is his usual forex sequence. Prior New York Stock Exchange lows were taken, he wanted most or all of a fair value gap filled, and price later did that, broke structure higher once New York was open, and he entered with the stop under those lows. The first target was the prior session high, marked from the candle body out to the wick. He says that target paid about 7.7 thousand dollars, then a wick ran the rest back to break-even. Other people in the room held into later targets built from Asian and London highs, again from body and from wick. His improvement is the small dip that often follows a break of structure. Entering there, he says, would have kept him in for those later targets.

The pound-dollar trade was the same sequence: prior New York lows, a break of structure at the open, stop under the lows. He says this particular sequence has not, for him, required an extra confluence beyond the break. A higher-time-frame order block sat under the idea, the move that expanded up after a quick drop. When he recorded, the trade was in drawdown and five-minute and fifteen-minute structure were still up. If it stopped, his review would be that the New York lows were only tapped, and that the daily trend deserved more weight. He had expected a relief push after a large dump, the same read as the prior day's pound-yen long.

The lesson is what people did while that pound trade went against them. One indecisive candle and one larger down candle showed up, structure on the five-minute was still up, and price had moved about 11 pips toward a 24-pip stop. That is under half the risk he had marked, and he says the room was already calling the trade a mistake and looking for the exit. His claim is that this reaction means the size is too large, or the person is not ready for a live account. He says the skill gets learned on a practice account until ordinary drawdown does not force an exit.

He treats the open as a job with about two focused hours, and he wants the other hours of the day to carry the jokes. Rolling out of bed into the bell is, for him, the same as walking into a game with no warmup. His own picture of the job is being awake hours early, clearing his head, and already knowing the news. A breakup, a death, or any day that has already wrecked the mood is a day he says to leave alone. He ties a recent low mood of his own to a small losing week. Chasing a win to repair that mood is the path he names toward revenge trades. At the end of the recording the pound trade was about halfway to the stop, and he says he was fine with that because gold had already hit a first target.

## What we learned

Day 8 is the opening of the topic he calls the center of his method: liquidity. The scope is narrow on purpose. He defines what it is and why he thinks it moves price. Marking the pools on a chart, and trading them, is outside this video.

Liquidity, in his words, is resting orders. That includes stop orders, which take someone out, and limit orders, which put someone in. The price is a pool: when it is traded, many of those orders activate at once, so a large amount of money changes hands there. His simple case is that a buy only fills if someone is selling. Large participants have size that a quiet price will not match, so he says they seek those pools in order to get filled and then move price the way they intend. He sets aside the story that price bounced off a floor or a ceiling, because that story gives no reason.

The reason he wants is a sequence already partly taught. Price trades into the pool, orders are filled, and a break of structure shows that direction has changed. Reaching the pool by itself is not the event. If the structure shift never prints, he says the correct act is to do nothing. He also says the student cannot see the exact moment a large order is pressed, so the structure shift is the evidence that the fill has happened. The assignment is to rewatch until that definition is clear.

## What to remember

- In this lesson, liquidity means resting orders, stops and limits, pooled at a price.
- A transaction needs the other side. Large size, he says, looks for a place where many opposing orders are waiting.
- Price touching that place is incomplete. The confirmation he names is the break of structure from the prior chart lesson.
- If that shift never prints, the lesson's stance is to leave the pool alone.
- This video stops at the definition and the reason. It does not show how the pools are marked.

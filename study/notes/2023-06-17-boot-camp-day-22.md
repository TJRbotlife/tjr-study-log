## What we learned

This is the spotting day. He tells people who were confused on the face-to-camera explanation to stop trying to draw it while he is only defining it. The first day was for listening and writing the description. The chart work starts here.

He restates the block as the move before the liquidity sweep, the move that causes the sweep, and the range that sits prior to the break of structure. That is where orders were filled so the break could happen. When price returns, the idea is that more orders can be filled in the same range, and price can leave again with the new direction. He shows it both after a rally that breaks a downtrend and after a selloff that breaks an uptrend.

On the chart, the block is the whole leg, not the single candle that is easiest to box. A four-hour candle can look precise and still be only part of a larger move. Scaling down makes the range usable, and the timeframe of the original move decides how much room the idea needs. A tight stop on an hourly break, when the structure shift was a four-hour move, is how he says people get run over. The target side of that same idea is prior liquidity on the higher timeframe.

He then nests timeframes on a GBP/USD downtrend. The daily leg is the broad block. A four-hour break tells him a retrace is underway. An imbalance that price blows through is dropped. He scales through the hourly into the fifteen-minute, waits for the New York open, and waits for a break of structure before he talks about a long. In the lesson's example he calls one path about a 1-to-12 reward multiple, and he says that path only exists if someone actually waited: daily break, return into the leg, then a lower-timeframe break. Entering at the first lower-timeframe break, before price came back into the block, is the impatient version he expects most people to take. A London-session example repeats the same nesting, with a fair value gap used only after it actually reacts.

Some marked blocks never get tapped. He says price can continue off some other reason, and that has to be acceptable. The homework is five examples of order blocks on three different timeframes, on whatever pairs are already being watched. When he is in a session, a break of structure makes him see the causing leg immediately, often without drawing it. If a sweep entry would fill him at a poor reward, that leg is the return he would rather wait for.

## What to remember

- Spotting starts from the causing leg, the whole move that swept liquidity, not from the prettiest single candle.
- The timeframe that created the block is the timeframe the room has to respect. A lower-timeframe stop on a higher-timeframe move is the error he keeps showing.
- A lower-timeframe break can be the retrace into the higher-timeframe block. He still waits for price to arrive there.
- A block that never gets tapped is a normal outcome in his examples.
- The practice he assigns is finding the legs on several timeframes until the causing move is visible without a drawing.

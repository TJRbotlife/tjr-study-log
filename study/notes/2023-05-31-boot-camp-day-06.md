## What we learned

Day 6 defines a break of structure as a change in the trend from Day 4. First he defines the swings that trend is made of. A high is a move up followed by a move down. A low is a move down followed by a move up. He says that can be as small as two candles, and it does not need a pretty shape. The high is the higher wick of those two candles. The low is the lower wick of the pair. Zooming into a lower time frame is how he shows why the extreme of the pair, and not the neighboring wick, is the mark.

A break to the upside, out of a downtrend, is a candle body closing above the most recent high, with that body above the high's wick. A break to the downside, out of an uptrend, is a candle body closing below the most recent low. A wick that pokes through the level leaves the trend intact. He shows several of those wicks as the places people misread a break, and he calls the same picture a liquidity sweep, which the liquidity days take up. Only the newest swing counts. Once a newer high or low has formed, the older one drops out of the watch.

He writes the check in order: name the trend, watch lows in an uptrend and highs in a downtrend, and require a close beyond the most recent one. A break on a smaller chart does not mean that direction runs forever. The weekly still outweighs the daily, and the daily outweighs the chart beneath it, so a daily break inside a still-bullish weekly trend can be a retracement. The assignment is ten real body-close breaks and ten wick-only lookalikes.

## What to remember

- A swing high is an up move followed by a down move, marked at the higher wick of that pair. A swing low is the reverse, marked at the lower wick.
- A break is a candle body closing beyond the most recent swing. A wick through that swing leaves the trend intact.
- When a newer swing forms, the older swing is no longer the one he watches.
- A break on a lower time frame, while the higher time frame's sequence is intact, is the smaller swing.
- The wick-through picture he rejects as a break is the picture he names a liquidity sweep.

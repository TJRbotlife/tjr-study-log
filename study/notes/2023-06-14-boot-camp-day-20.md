## What we learned

This is the explanation day for order blocks. He says the first lesson in a building-block series is why the idea exists. This video stays on that definition. He leaves the day without homework.

An order block, in his definition, is the move that creates a liquidity sweep. Taking a prior high requires a move up. Taking a prior low requires a move down. That leg is the price range where orders were filled, and the break of structure that follows is what shifts the market. The name is literal for him: the block is where those orders got filled.

He treats the later return the way he treats a fair value gap. If shorts were filled on the way through that range, a later push back into the same range is where more orders can be filled in the opposite direction, and price can leave again the way the new structure points. The same story runs in reverse after a sweep of a low.

He ranks the tools. A liquidity sweep plus a break of structure is the start of the move. Fair value gaps, order blocks, and equilibrium are the retracement ideas. If the first entry is missed, the order block is his first place to look for a return, because it still sits at the origin of the move and can be a tighter location than the sweep itself. The gap comes next in his order, then equilibrium.

One trend contains one order block. It forms off the sweep, the filled orders, and the new direction. The next block appears when that trend shifts again. He says the same idea exists on every timeframe. The leg before the sweep is the whole definition he wants remembered: orders filled there, structure broke because of that, and price often wants to revisit the range.

He closes by pointing at that session's PPI and FOMC tape. On the fifteen-minute, from the U.S. open, GBP/JPY had moved about 34 pips in the lesson's example, which he offers as a picture of a market that is not offering clean conditions.

## What to remember

- The block is the leg that swept liquidity and filled orders, sitting just before the break of structure.
- A later visit to that range is where he expects more orders to be filled, in the direction of the new structure.
- In his ranking it is the first retracement tool, ahead of the gap, because it remains at the start of the move.
- One trend, one block. A new block belongs to the next shift, and each timeframe can have its own.
